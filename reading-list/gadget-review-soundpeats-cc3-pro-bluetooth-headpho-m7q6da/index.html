Gadget Review: SoundPEATS CC3 Pro Bluetooth Headphones ★★★★⯪

shkspr.mobi · 2026年10月28日

These are easily the most comfortable headphones I've worn. I've literally had them clipped around my ears all day without noticing them.

The good folks at SoundPEATS have sent me their latest cans to wrap around my lugs. I'm a big fan of these new-style "clip on" headphones. Rather than encasing your ears, or sticking way down into the wax-mines, these clip over your ear. It isn't a tight grip like a paperclip - more like the gentle caress of a tender but firm lover who wants only to delicately pinch your ears.

Look - it's hard writing about this stuff, OK?! Grab a pair and try them; you'll see what I mean. Here's a picture of an attractive model to give you an idea.

Specs

All the usual things you'd expect from headphones. AAC and SBC audio codecs are standard, it'll also do LDAC - which will get you higher quality but at the expense of battery life and the ability to connect to multiple devices at once.

The case recharges via USB-C, the battery seems fairly chunky and will quick-charges the headphones. 10 minutes in the case will give them 3 hours of playback.

Each clip has a button - you can change what they do in the supplied app.

IPX6 should be good for wearing in the rain, but you can't go swimming in them.

Useful Features

We all know what headphones can do - play audio, provide a microphone, and let you control the audio coming from your device. The CC3 Pro also have a few other smart features.

Like most decent battery-powered headphones, they will automatically switch off after a predetermined period without use.

There's a privacy mode which adds an EQ filter to reduce sound leakage. It works, but does make the sound slightly murkier.

There's a button on each one - you can use the app to set actions for tap, double-tap, triple-tap, and long press.

The case supports wireless charging, so you can chuck it on any charging puck for a quick top-up.

Comfort

Outstanding. After a few minutes you will genuinely forget that you're wearing them. Shake your head as hard as you like and they will not fall off. Here's a genuine photo of me doing some manly exercises while wearing them.

Sound Quality

Excellent - with a couple of caveats. These are open-ear devices. You can hear the world around you. That is by design and is extremely useful if you need to be aware of your surroundings. You can't block out the world or do active noise cancelling. I was not a fan of the default EQ. The bass wasn't bassy enough for me. It's easy enough to change in the app - but that does require a login.

Voice quality when speaking on the phone was also good. The people I spoke to could hear me clearly and with minimal background noise.

Android

Piece of cake. Open the case, press the button on the back, they're ready to pair.

Sound quality was great, my voice transmitted just fine, and I could easily play/pause and adjust the volume.

Linux

Again, dead easy. Ubuntu detected and paired with them instantly.

The App

I've complained to SoundPEATS before about their lousy app. The best thing I can say about it is that you don't have to use it. It's handy for downloading the occasional firmware update and fiddling with the setting. But that's about it.

It obnoxiously insists on needing an Internet connection to change some settings. Stuck on a train in the middle of nowhere and want to adjust your EQ? Tough. Similarly, some functionality is gated behind a login wall - such as the custom EQ and custom sound effects.

Thankfully, the rest of the app is fairly simple to use and will work offline. You can adjust what the buttons do, for example:

Weirdly, you can also download other people's EQ settings.

There's also a firmware update and a bunch of other things to flick on and off.

Downsides

Other than the app, there are a few niggles which are only slightly annoying.

Firstly, they don't pause when you take them out of your ear. That's a feature which was on the first set of SoundPeats I had and is sorely missed.

Secondly, the default sound profile wasn't very bass heavy. A bit of fiddling in the app and they sounded much better.

While they do charge in either slot within the case, they're incompatible with previous SoundPeats cases. The risk with smaller manufacturers like this is that you won't be able to find a cheap replacement case if you need it.

Price

Launch price is about £75 / €80. That's getting towards the upper end of what I think is acceptable for headphones like this.

Readers of this blog can use code PRCC3PRO for an extra 10% off at Amazon UK or other international Amazon stores. The code should work from today until 30th November 2026.

Ultimately, these are ridiculously comfortable headphones. They won't block out the noise of the environment around you - but that's their selling point. You use these while cycling, wandering through the city, or any other time you need to be aware of your surrounding. If you can put up with the slightly shonky app, these are well worth the price.

Many thanks to SoundPeats for the review pair.
